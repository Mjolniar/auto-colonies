# Jonah's Colony Manager

Colony management automation (Colony Autopilot) for Starsector 0.98a. No balance changes: it only manages your colonies (battle size and sticky combat autopilot left in 2.7.0).

## Install

1. Put the `Jonah's Colony Manager` folder in `Starsector/mods/`.
2. Enable **Jonah's Colony Manager** in the launcher. Its mod id is still `jonah_tweaks`, so saves that used the old name keep working. No required dependencies. Optional integrations load only when their mod is enabled: Terraforming Made Easy, Industrial Evolution, Nexerelin, Emergent Threats, Ashes of the Domain (Building Menu Overhaul; Vaults of Knowledge research, synergies, management styles, megastructures and items; Theory of Toolbox trade contracts), Random Assortment of Things (Frontiers settlement), Station Augments, Terraforming & Station Construction, Grand.Colonies, Substance.Abuse, Volkov Industrial Conglomerate, Roider Union, Planetary Shield: Access Control, Unknown Skies, What We Left Behind, Astral Ascension, Omega Core Everywhere, Astral Ascension Renewed, Niko's Scavengable Industries, Junk Yard Dogs, Carter's Freetraders, Vulpoids, Yunru Industries, Slightly Better Tech-Mining. Any combination works; see "Building from source".
3. Defaults for new saves are in `data/config/colony_autopilot.json`. Every option can also be changed in-game.
4. Don't remove the mod from a save that has used it: Autopilot Storage is a market tab in your colonies.

## Using it

- **Colony panel:** the **Colony Autopilot** button on the colony screen, or right-click **Population & Infrastructure**, then **Colony Autopilot...** The button finds a free spot near the building grid (with a full grid, anywhere along the rows under and above it or beside it) and doesn't appear if there's none. It's only looked for on the colonies tab and the docked market screen, so it never lands on the refit or fleet screens (where Jonah's Auto Outfitting has its own button) and those screens aren't searched at all. It sits in the colony screen's own panel, so it moves and hides with it, and it moves when the buildings change or you switch colonies; setting **Button on the colony screen** turns it off. Pages listed on the left: Overview (stats, approval, goal, mode, fast buildout), Plan and builds, Locks, and Mod features (every mod feature that applies to the colony, on one page).
- **Empire panel:** Intel screen, the **Colony Autopilot** filter (its own tag). Pages listed on the left: Colonies (budget, approvals, every colony), Autopilot Storage, Colony crises, Mod features (every mod feature in use, on one page), Settings, Log, Diagnostics. Settings groups open one at a time.
- **Modes per colony:** Off, Advisor (default: recommends, you click Build), Approve (one-click proposals), Auto (builds within your budget).
- **Goals:** Balanced, Money, Security, War production, Rapid growth, Self-sufficiency. Each colony gets a plan from its planet, hazards and resources. With Terraforming Made Easy, that includes a reachable special world (Forge, Fortress, Paradise, Hive, Death, Waste) resolved from TME's own requirements. A world is only picked if its industries, with the colony's other industries, fit the slots the colony gets at its max size (1 slot to size 3, then 2, 3 and 4 at sizes 4 to 6, plus any bonus it has), and a year of what its end conditions do (hazard on upkeep and growth, deposits) counts, so a world that keeps or adds hazards loses to one that clears them. TME's structures and the Unification Center cost more than they earn, but on a plan they get the plan's priority anyway, since they're how the world is reached. A colony with no reachable world keeps a plain plan: no world steps, only hazard clean-up. A step research blocks for now (the building the colony must add next is locked) gives up its rank, so the next step that can be built gets the most, and a step whose own building is locked passes its rank to what it's upgraded from (Smelting on the way to Refining). A new industry the plan doesn't ask for counts 30,000 a month less while the free industry slots are all the plan's industries have left.
- **Fast buildout:** after the first colony is founded, a popup offers to build it up fast: Auto, starting the fastest-paying builds (value per credit) daily with everything above a credit floor (100k, 250k, 500k or 1M; 50k-2M in the colony panel). It ranks by money only (the Money goal's weights with ground defense, fleets and ship quality worth nothing, and no plan or upgrade bonus), whatever the colony's goal and even while the threat is high, so it never builds defenses, patrols or stations. The monthly budget, per-project cap, pause on loss and new-upkeep cap don't apply; fees, the stability floor, locks and the measured-building approval do. It ends after 30 days in a row of positive colony net income, leaving the colony in Auto with its own goal. The colony panel can stop it, change the floor, or start one for any colony that loses money.
- **First switch to Approve or Auto** opens the setup page, pre-filled with the recommended settings: everything that pays for itself is on, and removals, story points and offense stay yours. **Use recommended settings** in the empire panel puts them back any time. Until setup runs, agents, autonomy, research, contracts and the settlement do nothing.
- **Orbital Fusion Lamp:** it cancels Cold, Very Cold, Poor Light and Dark, but always needs 10 volatiles (a shortage adds up to +50% hazard), and on any world that isn't cold it adds heat: Hot, or Extreme Heat in place of Hot. Autopilot places it only where the cold or darkness it cancels is worth more than the heat it adds, or where the colony's plan needs Extreme Heat (Terraforming Made Easy's ore projects take it in place of extreme tectonic activity). A lamp at an Auto colony where it does neither goes back to Autopilot Storage, one a day, and the game puts the colony's old heat back.
- **Market model** (2.44.0, `CAMarket`, setting `noMarketModel` inverted): `CAEngine.supply` values a line by the export income it adds (share = min(supply, available, shipping) x accessibility over all exporters, times the good's market value and the colony's income multiplier, as CommodityMarketData does), only above the colony's largest single source, plus shortage relief at the flat rate; picks started are claimed (`CAData.marketPending`) until built; a pass skips a pick mostly worth goods another colony just claimed; Roider and Yunru drydocks pass whole output when the model is on (`CAEngine.callerNets`). Report section `market`, weekly self-check.
- **Oddisz's mods** (2.45.0, `CAOddisz`, `CALuna`): Cloning's three tiers valued in CAMods.apply from BaseCloning's growth formula against CAPolicies.growth (fx[6] = 2 x speed-up, at most 6), -size stability, never at max size or without room for the penalty to grow a size; the Reverse Engineering Hub is in CAMods.LEFT, and LEFT buildings are skipped by item placement, swaps, Low profile's item pull, research's item spending, CAOverLimit.weakest and CAPrune; Sol Renewed's crisis (CrisisIntel, $sr_crisis_ref) prices an industry in a $sr_sol system at cost/50,000 points a month x a wave's share (CACrisis.CRISIS_COST x 1.5 / 183), shown on the Crises page; CACompat entries for Cloning, the Private Arsenal, Coalesced Little Mods, Toolbox Economy Curves and Sol Renewed.
- **Low profile** (2.43.0, `CALowProfile`, setting `lowProfile`, picked in the first colony's dialog): `CACrisis.pointCost` x10; `CAItems.coreLimit` 0 above size 3 while the Hegemony counts and under the Pather line (`pathLimit`) while the Pathers do; `CAEngine.Pick.lowProfile` caps a pick at -1 when it takes interest to 7 (`score`, items in `placements`/`swapItem`/`idleBonus`); Tri-Tachyon competing exports (`ttProgress` as the game), Diktat fuel from any building and Luddic majorities priced; daily `run` pulls items over the line and swaps an AI administrator for an idle human one; free ports closed (`CAPolicies`), no attacker raids (`CAWar`), no offense agents (`CASpies.offense`), no Omega/IX cores, no RAT settlement by a Remnant nexus, inspections never resisted. Each rule lifts when that faction's crisis is over for good.
- **Research, one at a time** (2.42.4, `CAResearch.run`): when the best tech's databanks have to be shipped to the facility (topUp ships, topUpPending), its id is kept in `researchStaging`, it goes first on later passes, and no other tech is shipped for or started until it starts; before, every try in the list was topped up in turn (five or six shipments and lines a pass) and a filler then spent the databanks moved for the first.
- **Update notifications** (2.42.3): `JonahsColonyManager.version` at the mod root, registered in `data/config/version/version_files.csv`, in Version Checker format (MagicLib's built-in checker, TriOS, other mod managers): masterVersionFile is the copy on GitHub main, directDownloadURL the latest release's fixed-name `JonahsColonyManager.zip`, changelogURL the patch notes. The build writes its version from mod_info.json and the release refuses a zip whose version file disagrees.
- **Stability guard** (2.42.2, `CAOverLimit`): the game decivilizes a colony at 0 stability for months (DecivTracker: monthly-ish samples over 16 months, half at 0 incl. the first and the last 3, one warning, then 10%/month per month past 3). Daily, every Auto colony (sieges included): over its industry limit at stability 2 or less, an industry goes at once whatever it earns (any industry but population, stations, improved, locked or being built; unvalued mod ones count as worth 0); at 0 for 20 days, the building with the most negative own stability modifier ("ind_<id>" flat mods) goes, one every 10 days, a shed capital step held off the plan a year; every colony at 0 (Auto or not) gets a monthly on-screen alert with its four worst stability modifiers. Capital trade steps wait under stability 3. Capital (2.42.2): a capital that is still a market but not yours (captured) keeps capitalAuto/capitalId; a stand-in takes the gathering point only, gathering is off, and the capital returns when it is yours. Logistics off: both hubs are the capital only (old logisticsHub/SellHub ignored). Agents (2.42.2): security takes over an idle agent of yours that can infiltrate cells instead of only asking you to hand it over; spyChecked turns Agents to Defense once in older saves that had them off.
- **Industry limit** (2.42.1, `CABuild.takesSlot`, `CAOverLimit`; setting **Fix colonies over the industry limit**, Yes by default): CABuild.start refuses a new industry, or an upgrade that turns a structure into one, when Misc.getNumIndustries is already at Misc.getMaxIndustries (picks are scored with the slots free when the pass began, and one pass starts a new building and an upgrade, so before this both could take the last slot). Daily, an Auto colony over its limit (vanilla: a flat -5 stability, and income x stability/5 below 5) takes down its weakest industry the engine can value (not building/upgrading, improved, locked, a station, population, a plan special step or capital step, a contract feeder, or with defense, fleet, quality or ship output), its core and item to Autopilot Storage, one per colony every 30 days; it keeps them when that industry is worth more than 5 stability there (CAItems.stabilityWorth; at stability 0 it always fixes it) or the colony is 80% of the way to a size with room.
- **Stations** (2.42.0, `CAStations`; setting **Station at every colony** under Building and planning, Yes by default): every colony out of its fast buildout at size 4 or more (or 180 days old) gets a station plan step in front of its own plan ("station": any station counts), plus its line's tier-2 station from size 6 and tier-3 from size 8 (5 and 7 at the capital). They count as enablers in CAEngine.finish (plan rank even when upkeep outweighs the goal's defense and fleet weights) and endgame keeps their plan bonus. Before, on Balanced, an Orbital Station scored about -2,000 a month (worth 5,700 minus 7,844 for its 250,000 price over three years), so only Security, threats, Fortress/Bastion plans or spending freely built one. The tier follows the colony's own station's upgrades, else the Station line setting, else low tech.
- **Capital** (2.42.0, `CACapital`; settings under Settings, **Capital**): one colony is your home port. Autopilot picks it once (the production gathering point scores 1,000, then Storage contents: 10 a mothballed ship up to 300, 2 a stack up to 200; size x10, accessibility x20, free industry slots x5, Auto +20) and keeps it; **Make this the capital** on a colony panel, the Settings row, or moving the gathering point yourself picks another. Daily it keeps the production gathering point there, so custom production is delivered to it, and storage logistics runs weekly for it even with Storage logistics off (**Gather your Storage there**, Yes by default): both hubs default to the capital and share one load. Once your colonies ramp up (no fast buildout at the capital and last month's net income past **Commerce starts**, 100,000 by default) its plan gets extra steps in front: Spaceport, Megaport, Commerce, Trade Outpost, Bazaar, Requisitions Centre, Union HQ, Salvage Yards, Underworld, Waystation (each only if the game has it). They count as enablers (plan rank even when they lose money), endgame never replaces them, and Yunru's Bazaar is scored only there. With every industry slot taken, the capital takes down its weakest industry the engine can value (never defenses, fleets, ship quality or ship output, mod buildings, locked, improved or other plans' special steps) for one, one every 90 days (**Make room for trade buildings**). While net income is below zero the steps wait.
- **Storage logistics** (2.39.0, `CALogistics`; setting under Settings, **Storage logistics**, off until you switch it on): your Storage tabs gathered in two places. The **refit hub** (a colony or market you pick; by default your production gathering point) gets, from every other Storage you have at a market that isn't hostile to you, what you refit a fleet with: mothballed ships (setting), weapons, fighter wings, blueprints and hull mod specs you don't know yet (one copy each), and supplies, fuel, crew and marines (setting). The optional **sell hub** gets blueprints and hull mod specs you already know (every hull, weapon, wing or building a blueprint or package teaches) and spare copies of ones you don't. Once a week one load from each Storage to each hub; each costs what an Autopilot Storage shipment does (2,000 plus 300 a light-year, times the fee level) plus 1,000 a ship, from the monthly budget, and arrives 2 days plus a day a light-year later in the hub's normal Storage (your biggest colony's if that Storage is gone). A load the budget can't pay for waits, with everything left where it was. Colony items, AI cores and Autopilot Storage are never touched. The Autopilot Storage page lists loads on their way.
- **Autopilot Storage** (per colony since 2.37.0): a tab at every colony market, each holding that colony's own store. Colony items, AI cores, Antimatter Stabilizers, research databanks, VPC chips and recipes deposited there are the only ones the manager uses. It uses an item at the colony it's stored at; anything it needs at another colony is **shipped** there first: a fee (2,000 plus 300 a light-year, times the convenience fee level, from the monthly budget) and travel time (2 days plus a day a light-year), delivered into that colony's Autopilot Storage, then installed. A core moved between colonies' buildings, a Stabilizer, a VPC chip and a recipe travel the same way; only one core is on its way to a colony at a time. Placement is best first (2.39.0, `CAItems.placements`/`assign`): every waiting item is matched with every free building it fits and the best-scoring pairs go first, each building to one item, each stack to as many buildings as it has copies; on a tie the copy already at that colony wins, and a building whose item still has to be shipped is held for it, so no second copy is shipped while the first waits to go in. VPC chips go building by building the same way (`CAIndEvo.installVPC`): one already at the colony first, none shipped while one is on its way. What the manager takes out of a building (a core it replaces, an item it pulls back, a building it takes down) goes into that colony's own Autopilot Storage. Before 2.37.0 it was one shared storage at every colony, so anything put in at one colony could be taken out at any other, and the manager installed anywhere: free transport across the sector. An older save's shared storage is moved into the biggest colony's, once, with a log line. Campaign consumables and blueprints (Industrial Evolution's consumables, ship and weapon blueprints, Random Assortment's Forge-Assisted Industry BP, anything its mod marks as a consumable or that's used up on right-click) can't go in; ones already there (2.36.1) go to that colony's normal Storage, with a log line. Since 2.39.2 special items are an allow-list (`CAStoragePlugin.usable`): only colony items the game can install (its item effects table), items the manager values by hand, VPC chips and Substance.Abuse recipes go in; anything else (blueprint packages, building blueprints, UAF's Access Key Cards) stays out and is moved to normal Storage once a day. The empire panel's Autopilot Storage page lists each colony's store, what's on its way, each colony's inspection points and, with Vaults of Knowledge, the Research section.
- **No cheats or balance changes** (2.37.0): the storage cheat (Autopilot Storage took anything and colonies drew their shortages from it for free) and the Derelict Empire start's "Attacks on your colonies" settings (fewer pirate and crisis fleets per system, Nexerelin attacks spaced out) are gone; both changed the game's rules. Their old setting fields stay in the save so it loads, unused. Goods stored under the cheat can still be taken out.

## Any mix of mods

Every part has a fallback, so the manager works with any combination of the supported mods, or none:

| Part | With the mod | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| Colony plans (Terraforming Made Easy) | A reachable special world: its industries must fit the slots the colony gets at max size, and its end conditions' hazard and deposits count; TME's structures and the Unification Center get the plan's priority | A plain plan for the goal (Forge, Fortress, Paradise, Bastion, Fuel Depot, Mining Colony, Trade Hub), vanilla buildings plus hazard clean-up; with TME on but no world reachable, the same without world steps |
| Plan steps | Any step whose building or condition the game has | A step from a mod that isn't installed is dropped, so it never takes a plan rank |
| Research locks (Ashes of the Domain, Building Menu Overhaul) | Locked steps are worked toward through research; AotD's own versions count through their upgrade links | Every vanilla building is open; nothing waits on research |
| Research (Vaults of Knowledge) | Techs ranked by what colony plans need through their whole chain, then by what they unlock; a Research Facility is worth building when plans wait on research and you have none; with research on Auto and none anywhere, one goes first in the focus world's plan, else the biggest Auto colony's (2.39.0, `CAResearch.stepsFor`); more from the surplus while the research left is worth the speed; Alpha cores into research buildings first | Skipped |
| Terraforming (TME, Boggled's TASC) | Projects scored by what they add or remove, plan conditions first | Skipped; hazard clean-up steps drop out with their buildings |
| Growth | Hazard pay for the colonies it speeds up most, within a share of income; growth counts in every goal | Same (vanilla) |
| Values | Formulas for vanilla buildings, estimates for known mod buildings, probe-colony measurements for the rest | A building that can't be valued is listed in Diagnostics and never built by Auto |

Whatever a colony's plan is blocked on, it still builds whatever is worth most on its own; the plan only adds priority. A plan step that can't be reached (no way to build it, or anything on the way to it, and no research Autopilot is running that would open it) for 60 days stops counting: no plan rank, no reserved industry slot, a log line; it counts again the day a way opens (2.38.0). A Terraforming Made Easy world whose own buildings are out of reach is dropped for a year, so the colony follows the plain plan for its goal; a world that needs Mining or Farming is never picked where they can't go. If Building Menu Overhaul can't be read, only what research would lock stays locked.

## Rules it follows

- **Only worthwhile builds:** Approve and Auto only start a build that pays for itself within about 3 years: its money and other effects, valued with the colony goal's weights, must beat its build cost spread over 3 years. They never fall back to the least-bad option, so a building with nothing to do (a Research Facility with no research, say) isn't built. The colony panel shows each option's money per month after upkeep and how its ranking is made up, and marks the ones that aren't worth building.
- **Nexerelin outposts** (Nexerelin's forward bases, which sit in an economy of their own and can't export) are left alone: nothing is built, upgraded, placed or switched there, fast buildout skips them, and their shortages don't count as your colonies'.
- **Budget:** spends within the monthly budget, per-project cap and credit reserve. Emergency defense fleets may pass the monthly budget, never the reserve. Budget types: **Automatic** (recommended: 50% of last month's net income plus 15% of the credits above the reserve at the start of the month, so a young colony that loses money still gets built and spending slows as savings fall), **Share of net income**, or **Fixed amount**. Whatever's left of a month's budget is **saved up** (2.39.0), up to six months' worth, so a building that costs more than one month's budget gets built once enough is saved (before, it waited until savings alone had grown enough, months with credits piling up and nothing built); an emergency that goes over the budget comes out of what's saved. Recommended reserve: a quarter of your credits at setup, 50,000-500,000; **Pause on loss** recommended off. Each pass a colony puts up its best two upgrades and best three new builds, so when the best has to wait (budget, caps) the next one goes instead of the colony standing still. **New upkeep cap per month:** how much new monthly upkeep Auto may take on in a month, so a wrong estimate or a drop in income can be caught before much permanent cost is locked in (every build already has to pay for itself after its upkeep). **Automatic** (default and recommended since 2.24.0; older saves switch once, with a log line) is 10% of last month's net income and at least 20,000, so it keeps up as income grows; **Fixed** is an amount you set. The Colonies page shows this month's new upkeep against it. A plan step whose upkeep alone is over the whole cap (a Terraforming Made Easy structure at a big colony, say) goes as the month's first new upkeep, so it waits a month at most; before 2.21.0 it waited forever and the colony with it.
- **Surplus investment** (setting, recommended over 3,000,000; saves from before 2.21.0 get it once, with a log line): credits over the line go into one **focus world**, to make it the best colony it can be. While there's a surplus the focus world may spend it past the monthly budget and the per-project cap, its new upkeep cap is 10% of net income, its builds and terraforming count their price at a quarter (so expensive buildings that pay off count), its best-value terraforming is paid from the surplus, and research counts its plan twice and facility upkeep at a quarter. Credits already promised to production aren't surplus: the game's custom production still to build and, with Ashes of the Domain, its production orders' remaining units (Jonah's Auto Outfitting's weapon orders go there), so a weapons order is never left unpaid. Autopilot picks the Auto colony with the most to build on (size, free industry slots, deposits, habitable, hazard, waiting colony items that fit, income, a reachable special world) and keeps it unless another is clearly better; pick it yourself with **Make this the focus world** on its colony panel's Overview. The empire panel's Colonies page says how much is over the line and where it goes.
- **Spending freely** (2.32.0; setting **Over 10,000,000 of surplus**, recommended **Every colony spends it**, also what older saves get; or **Focus world only**): past 10,000,000 over the line one focus world can't spend it all (it's built out, and builds one new building at a time), so every Approve and Auto colony spends from the surplus: past the monthly budget and the per-project cap, prices counted at a quarter, and credits counting a quarter in what a building or upgrade is worth, so whatever adds defense, stability, growth, accessibility or fleet strength goes up even when it costs more than it earns. Everything it spends comes from the pool, not from income: the month's new upkeep may reach a sixtieth of the surplus (the pool covers five years of it), shrinking as the pool does; hazard pay goes wherever it speeds growth by 5% or more, with no budget limit; the plan's own best-value terraforming at every colony is paid from the surplus; and surplus terraforming starts up to three projects a day at a colony, on different structures, planned from what the colony will be once the running ones finish and never touching a running project's condition. It ends (logged) under 10,000,000 over the line; a weak month doesn't stop it (2.34.1: before, it paused while net income was under half its best month, and capped new upkeep at a fifth of net income). The Colonies page says when it's on and this month's upkeep room.
- **Endgame economy** (2.36.0; setting **Endgame economy** under Budget and spending: **From 1,000,000/month** (default, also older saves), From 2,000,000/month, From 500,000/month or Off; **Endgame replaces industries**, default On): while spending freely with last month's net income at that level, money stops being the goal at every colony that spends the surplus. A credit counts 5% in what a building, upgrade, AI core or item is worth; stability, access, defense, fleets, ship quality and growth count at least 4,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 / 40,000 / 40,000 / 2,000 (or the colony goal's own weight if higher). Every good a building makes is valued as a production chain: a good none of your colonies make is worth 40,000 plus 4,000 a unit; units that cover your own colonies' shortfall 3,000 each; the rest 1,000 a unit times the share of the sector's production that isn't yours yet (toward a monopoly); a second colony making a good only one makes adds 10,000, so production spreads over your worlds. War goods (ships, weapons, supplies, fuel, heavy machinery, metals, transplutonics, marines) count 1.5x, Ashes of the Domain's advanced goods 2x. Buildings under construction count as made, so two colonies don't start the same missing good. The plan only adds priority where nothing else would get there: Terraforming Made Easy's structures and special-world buildings, special project producers, maxed stations and defenses, the Bifrost Control Center; a money plan's Commerce or Light Industry is valued like anything else. Research Facilities go up while any research is left. With every industry slot taken, an Auto colony takes down its weakest industry when the best one it has no slot for is worth 10,000 a month more, then builds that (one a day across the empire, one a month per colony). Never replaced: military industries (fleets, ship quality, defense, ship production), plan, project, research and wonder buildings, story-point improvements, locked buildings, mod buildings the engine can't value for certain, anything it built in the last year, a good's last producer while your colonies would be short, and a building making a good one of your trade contracts needs while the contract runs (2.39.0, `CAContracts.feeds`). A building it took down isn't built there again for a year. The old building's AI core and item go to Autopilot Storage to be placed again. Income: net income may fall to a third of what it was when the endgame started (3M a month keeps 1M); each month's builds that earn less draw on that room and wait once it's used ("waits: it costs ... of income and the endgame keeps ..."). It ends under 5,000,000 of surplus or when net income goes below zero, and comes back while spending freely at the income it keeps. The Colonies page's budget lines say when it's on, the income it keeps, the room left this month, the goods the sector makes that you don't, and how many buildings it replaced this year.
  - **Going all out** (2.33.0): a build's price counts 5% (tens of millions have nothing better to do), so anything that adds something gets built. Every colony maxes its structures, which need no industry slot: its station to the top of its line (up to a star fortress), Heavy Batteries, a Planetary Shield, a Megaport and a Patrol HQ are first plan steps, built even when they earn less than they cost. Colonies re-plan daily instead of weekly and start up to three upgrades at once (one per building). Surplus terraforming picks what adds most a month, not most per credit, so it goes for plentiful and ultrarich deposits instead of trace ones; tectonic activity may go in first when the richer ore or transplutonics it unlocks need it (TME's rule: abundant and up need extreme tectonic activity or a very hot world), and the plan doesn't clean the hazard up until the deposit is in (or 540 days pass). The producers of AotD's own goods (Refined Metal, Enriched Transplutonics, Electronic Components, Domain-grade machinery) get planned even with no special project running, since AotD's wonders, megastructure restoration, Bifrost gates and production all take them from your exports and nothing else makes them.
- **Surplus terraforming** (Terraforming Made Easy; setting, recommended **Max output**, also what saves from before 2.26.0 get): what the focus world leaves of the surplus goes into TME projects at every Auto colony, most value per credit first: richer deposits and farmland, harsh conditions cleared (a planet with none left turns habitable and gains farmland and organics, as TME does), building the structure first when the colony has none. It plans on TME's own rules one or two projects ahead, so an enabler (tectonic activity before richer transplutonics) counts with what it allows, and a project that loses more than it gains never goes: very hot or extreme tectonic activity, which the richest ore and transplutonics need, take a habitable world's habitability, farmland and organics, so a world like that keeps them. Nothing the colony's plan needs is undone and nothing it wants gone is added (the same rule as the plan's own projects, see **Terraforming**). TME's projects toggle a condition (the same project removes it from a world that has it and adds it to one that doesn't), so a plan is redone as soon as the colony's conditions change, and a project that would undo what the colony already is never starts. Before 2.30.0 it kept a plan for the whole session and started "remove" projects on worlds the plan's own project had just cleaned, which added pollution back at Gargor and high gravity at Hiverne and Zendia, round after round. A structure it builds is taken down once nothing worth doing is left for it (TME's changes stay), so its upkeep counts only while it works; structures you or the plan built stay. **Max output** funds anything worth 1,000 a month or more; **Pays back in 10 years** only what earns its price back by then; **Off**. Paid from the surplus only, outside the monthly budget, so ordinary builds never wait for it. Structures that research still locks (Mineral Replicator, Element Synthesizer, Agricultural Laboratory, Terrestrial Engine with Vaults of Knowledge) aren't built, but research values their techs by what they'd allow. The Stellar Manufactory and the Unification Center are left to you and the plan. The empire panel's Colonies page shows the best move, or what research would open.
- **Surplus research** (Vaults of Knowledge; setting, recommended **On**, also what saves from before 2.29.0 get): the surplus also builds Research Facilities at Auto colonies that have no research building, one at a time, up to the 8 colonies VoK counts (each past the first is +10% research speed). - **Terraforming:** hazards a project adds are scored, and side effects (TME removes a new condition's conflicts) are simulated in planning. A project that's a step of the colony's plan, the special world's final project included, goes past the per-project cap and the monthly terraforming budget: it's paid from your credits above the reserve, and waits (logged) until you have that much.

Since 2.30.0; before, only the condition itself counted.

Building a Heavy Industry or upgrading a forge counts as polluting only when a polluting nanoforge is in it or waiting in Autopilot Storage (AotD's no_pollution items don't).

  - A colony over its limit (its station lost, the next inspection stronger, the setting changed) sends its least useful core back to Autopilot Storage, one a day. Cores it can't move (an AI core administrator, locked buildings) are logged once, and no more go there.
  - A colony an inspection is planned against or on its way to is left as it is: the inspectors expect the cores they saw, and finding fewer disrupts every building there. A colony a Pather attack is planned against drops to under 7 Pather interest instead, one core a day, so its cell can dissolve before the attack fires.

One is moved to another Auto colony when they'd add at least 5,000 a month (or a quarter of their salary) more there. - **Siege:** a colony that's blockaded, has hostile fleets at the planet, has hostiles in its system that outweigh its defenses (the same rule as the emergency defense fleet), or has a hostile raid, invasion or blockade on its way, is under siege until that ends (checked daily). The log says when a siege starts and ends, and the colony panel says why.
- **Colony crises:** the game's crisis meter (Hostile Activity) gains every faction's progress each month, at most 60, times your best military structure (Patrol HQ x0.9, Military Base x0.7, High Command x0.5). At 500 the next crisis is picked, weighted by each faction's share; at 600 it fires and the meter falls back to 0-400. A month with no progress empties it. Autopilot prices progress at about 640 a month per point (a crisis roughly every 467 points, each held to cost 300,000):
  - AI cores pay for the Hegemony progress and Pather interest they add (above). A Patrol HQ, Military Base or High Command that would become your best military structure counts what it slows. Fuel Production counts the Sindrian Diktat progress it adds (the Diktat counts your largest fuel output from 4, once a fuel producer is size 4 or more).

Raids that meet in-system defenses about 1.34 times their strength while you're away abort, and that ends pirate, Pather, Diktat and Tri-Tachyon crises for good. The log says once which crisis is coming and where.
  - A coming Pather attack pulls cores from its target (above). A coming Nexerelin police raid on free ports closes the free ports of Auto colonies that would count (size + drugs + weapons - 2, from 5).
  - Deals, fights and memberships stay yours. The empire panel's **Colony crises** page shows the meter, the coming crisis and its likely target, your military multiplier, each faction's monthly progress with what drives it, how it ends and what Autopilot does about it, and the Hegemony's inspections so far. Nothing skips the game's rules: no avert helpers or flags.

Hazard pay adds a colony's hazard penalty back plus 5 growth points a month, at 1,000 credits a point, and a colony needs 300 x 2^(size-3) points for its next size. Auto colonies are ranked by how much faster it makes them grow (its points against their own growth; a colony that barely grows gains most), and the best ones get it while it speeds them up by at least **Hazard pay where growth is at least** (25%) and all of it fits within **Hazard pay limit, all colonies** (30% of net income before hazard pay). The more you earn, the more colonies get it. One already paying keeps it with a little leeway, so it doesn't flip; it goes off in a money emergency, at max size, or when the budget goes to colonies it speeds up more. The log gives the growth and the months to the next size with and without it. When one colony's hazard pay costs more than the whole budget, the log says so with both numbers. Saves from before 2.20.0 still on the old default of 10% move to 30% once (logged).

The estimate uses hostile factions nearby (at most 60% on their own), the Hostile Activity crisis, and raid, blockade, Pather cell and rebellion conditions. The state is kept in the save.

## Staying working through mod updates

So many mods are covered that some will update. These five safeguards keep an update from quietly breaking Autopilot:

1. **Health check at load.** Each integration knows its mod, the version it was written against, the ids it relies on and the names it reads. Diagnostics shows each one's status:
   - **OK**
   - **Newer version, all found:** it keeps working, and its estimates are trusted a little less.
   - **Partly off:** the parts whose ids are gone can't act.
   - **Off:** its code can't be read, so it does nothing.
2. **Mod values read live.** Where a mod exposes its numbers (Roider's mining tables and ranges, What We Left Behind's ore bonus, AotD's restoration rate), Autopilot uses the mod's own values. Its copies are only a fallback. A value that changed is logged once and listed in Diagnostics.
3. **Calibration from your colonies.** Once a week, every plain building you have (working, with no AI core, item or improvement) is compared with Autopilot's prediction: upkeep for every building type, and output for buildings scored from estimates. The ratio is smoothed (70% old, 30% new), capped at 0.5x to 2x, used once a type has two readings, and kept in the save. Estimates follow a mod's rebalance this way.
4. **Margins for uncertain picks.** The game's own formulas count in full. Probe measurements count at 90%, and estimates at 85% (92% once calibrated), minus 5% on a mod version an integration wasn't written for. So an estimate must beat a known option by about 18% to be picked. In Auto, a pick scored from an estimate that costs over half the per-project cap waits for your approval.
5. **Stopping actions that don't work.** After each action that reaches into the game or another mod (a build, an item install, a research or terraforming start, a megastructure setting, a restoration, a contract), Autopilot checks that it took effect. Three failures in a row pause that action for the session, with a log line and a Diagnostics entry. Reloading tries again.

## Building from source

Source is in `src/`. It compiles against the game and optional mod jars with JDK 17:

```
javac --release 17 -cp "<core>/starfarer.api.jar;<core>/starfarer_obf.jar;<core>/fs.common_obf.jar;<core>/log4j-1.2.9.jar;<core>/json.jar;<core>/lwjgl.jar;<core>/lwjgl_util.jar;<TME>/TerraformingMadeEasy.jar;<IndEvo>/jars/IndEvo.jar;<Nexerelin>/jars/ExerelinCore.jar" -d build (all .java files under src)
jar cf jars/jonahtweaks.jar -C build data
```


The game's script class loader refuses any mod class that names `java.lang.reflect` or file classes ("File access and reflection are not allowed to scripts"), and desktop checks don't see that. All reflection goes through `CARefl`, which reaches `getMethod`, `invoke`, `getField`, `get` and `set` through method handles and never names the reflection classes. `check_banned.ps1` fails the build if any class refers to a class the game's loader bans. `JTLoaderCheck` loads every class under that loader and runs `CARefl`.

`CATerraform`, `CAIndEvo`, `CACourier`, `CADefense`, `CASettlement` and `CAAugments` reference optional mods. They're only called when that mod is enabled, so the mod runs without them. `CASettlement` and `CAAugments` also need the Kotlin runtime (LazyLib) and LunaLib on the compile classpath.

The author's build checks prove any combination works: jdeps shows only those classes reference each optional mod, every caller checks `isModEnabled`, and the jar links under `-Xverify:all` against all 32 combinations of the compiled-in optional mods. `CASynergies`, `CAContracts`, `CAHypershunt`, `CAMegastructures`, `CAResearch` and `CADevelopment` reach Vaults of Knowledge and Theory of Toolbox through reflection only (via `CARefl`), like `CAAotD`; if a name can't be found they're left out and the log says why. `JTAotDEconLinkCheck.java` checks those names; run it with `-XX:+UnlockDiagnosticVMOptions -XX:-BytecodeVerificationRemote` like the game's own launch flags, since some VoK classes refer to Sins of the Past classes that aren't installed. An offline check covers the augment choice (knapsack against brute force, swap margin, shrunk budget, incompatible pairs). `CAAotD` reaches Building Menu Overhaul through reflection only, so it needs no jar at compile time.

The colony-mod integrations (`CATasc`, `CAGrand`, `CABrewery`, `CAMods`) use literal ids, reflection or method handles only, so no jar is needed. TASC is reached through `MethodHandles.findStatic`, because plain reflection on its `boggledTools` needs Illustrated Entities' classes. `JTColonyModsLinkCheck.java` checks those names against the TASC and LunaLib jars.
