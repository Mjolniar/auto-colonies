# Low profile

By default the manager goes all out. It builds whatever pays the most, stuffs AI cores into everything the Hegemony won't inspect, opens free ports, and treats the attention all that draws as just another cost. That's great for money, but it means a steady stream of colony crises: inspections, Pather cells, Tri-Tachyon's mercenaries, the Diktat getting mad about your fuel, the Knights of Ludd showing up, and fleets you have to go deal with.

**Low profile** is the other way to play it. Your colonies still grow and still make money, but the manager keeps them under the radar and avoids as much conflict as the game lets it.

## Turning it on

When you found your first colony, the manager asks how you want to expand: **Full expansion** or **Low profile**. You can switch any time under **Settings, Expansion style** (it's the first group). It works on every colony in Auto, and colonies replan as soon as you flip it.

## What it changes

Every colony crisis in the game builds up from something. Low profile goes after each one:

- **Attention costs ten times as much.** The manager already puts a price on every point of colony crisis progress. In Low profile that price is ten times higher, so anything that draws attention has to earn a lot more to be worth it. The flip side: a Patrol HQ, Military Base or High Command slows *every* crisis, so those are worth ten times as much too, and get built a lot sooner.
- **Hegemony inspections.** No AI cores in colonies above size 3, which is where the Hegemony starts counting them. Cores already in there go back to Autopilot Storage, one a day. An AI core administrator gets swapped out for a human one once you have one free (the core goes back to Storage). Small colonies can still use cores.
- **Pather cells.** A colony gets a Pather cell once it's size 4 or more with 7 or more Pather interest. Low profile never builds, installs or slots anything that takes a colony to 7. That covers AI cores, most colony items, Tech-Mining on big ruins, Mining plus Refining plus an item, and so on. If items are what's keeping a colony over the line, they go back to Storage, the least useful first. Buildings never get torn down for it; it just stops adding more there.
- **Tri-Tachyon.** Tri-Tachyon sends mercenaries once your colonies make two or more of the goods it sells (4 or more units from a size 4+ colony). Low profile prices that in. One competing good is free, so it'll still do one; past that, a building has to really be worth it.
- **The Sindrian Diktat.** Making 4 or more fuel draws the Diktat. That was already priced into Fuel Production; now it's priced in for any building that makes fuel, at the Low profile rate.
- **The Luddic Church.** A habitable colony with Farming or Light Industry and no industrial or military building gets a Luddic majority, and the Knights of Ludd's crisis can end with that colony going to the Church. Low profile prices that in. A building that ends one, or keeps one from coming (Mining, Refining, Heavy Industry, Fuel Production, a Military Base or High Command), gets credit for it. A Patrol HQ doesn't count, the game doesn't see it as military.
- **No free ports.** A free port draws Nexerelin's police. Low profile closes them (when the manager handles free ports, which it does by default).
- **No starting fights.** No war fleet raids on other factions' colonies (defense fleets, relief and strikes on pirate and Pather bases still go). Agents do security and relations, never sabotage. Hegemony inspections are never resisted, since resisting makes the Hegemony hostile.
- **No Omega or IX cores** from the mods that add them.
- **No settlements next to a Remnant nexus** (Random Assortment of Things). A nexus crisis only ends when you blow up the nexus.

## When it lets up

Each rule only sticks around while that faction can still send its crisis. Once it's over for good, Low profile goes back to full expansion as far as that faction goes:

- **Hegemony:** you beat three of their inspections, or join the Persean League. AI cores are allowed again.
- **Luddic Path:** you beat their attack, or pay the tithe when a Pather fleet asks. Pather interest stops mattering.
- **Tri-Tachyon:** you raid their commerce. (Dealing with their mercenaries already cuts it to a quarter.)
- **Sindrian Diktat:** you beat their attack or take their deal.
- **Luddic Church:** you beat the Knights or take the Church's deal.

The Crises page in the empire panel shows which rules are still on.

## What it can't avoid

Two crises come from colony size alone, so nothing you build or skip avoids them:

- **Pirates.** These grow with your biggest colony, low stability and pirate bases nearby. Low profile builds military structures sooner, every colony gets a station anyway, the colony a crisis is coming for plans with Security, and war fleets (with Nexerelin) still hit pirate bases near you. Beat one big pirate raid and pirate crises are done for good.
- **The Persean League.** Once you've got a size 5 colony (or three colonies with one at size 4), the League wants you to join and blockades you until you do. Joining at Kazeron ends it, and it ends the Hegemony's crisis too. The manager won't do that for you, it's your call. It'll tell you once when the League starts counting.

## What it costs you

Less money. No AI cores in your bigger colonies, fewer colony items in places the Pathers care about, fewer rich industries, and no free port income. How much less depends a lot on your colonies. In exchange you spend a lot less time flying around putting out fires.
