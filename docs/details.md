# Jonah's Colony Manager

Colony management automation (Colony Autopilot) for Starsector 0.98a. No balance changes: it only manages your colonies (battle size and sticky combat autopilot left in 2.7.0).

## Install

1. Put the `Jonah's Colony Manager` folder in `Starsector/mods/`.
2. Enable **Jonah's Colony Manager** in the launcher. Its mod id is still `jonah_tweaks`, so saves that used the old name keep working. No required dependencies. Optional integrations load only when their mod is enabled: Terraforming Made Easy, Industrial Evolution, Nexerelin, Emergent Threats, Ashes of the Domain (Building Menu Overhaul; Vaults of Knowledge research, synergies, management styles, megastructures and items; Theory of Toolbox trade contracts), Random Assortment of Things (Frontiers settlement), Station Augments, Terraforming & Station Construction, Grand.Colonies, Substance.Abuse, Volkov Industrial Conglomerate, Roider Union, Planetary Shield: Access Control, Unknown Skies, What We Left Behind, Astral Ascension. Any combination works; see "Building from source".
3. Defaults for new saves are in `data/config/colony_autopilot.json`. Every option can also be changed in-game.
4. Don't remove the mod from a save that has used it: Autopilot Storage is a market tab in your colonies.

## Using it

- **Colony panel:** the **Colony Autopilot** button on the colony screen, or right-click **Population & Infrastructure**, then **Colony Autopilot...** The button finds a free spot near the building grid (with a full grid, anywhere along the rows under and above it or beside it) and doesn't appear if there's none. It's only looked for on the colonies tab and the docked market screen, so it never lands on the refit or fleet screens (where Jonah's Auto Outfitting has its own button) and those screens aren't searched at all. It sits in the colony screen's own panel, so it moves and hides with it, and it moves when the buildings change or you switch colonies; setting **Button on the colony screen** turns it off. Pages listed on the left: Overview (stats, approval, goal, mode, fast buildout), Plan and builds, Locks, and Mod features (every mod feature that applies to the colony, on one page).
- **Empire panel:** Intel screen, the **Colony Autopilot** filter (its own tag). Pages listed on the left: Colonies (budget, approvals, every colony), Autopilot Storage, Colony crises, Mod features (every mod feature in use, on one page), Settings, Log, Diagnostics. Settings groups open one at a time.
- **Modes per colony:** Off, Advisor (default: recommends, you click Build), Approve (one-click proposals), Auto (builds within your budget).
- **Goals:** Balanced, Money, Security, War production, Rapid growth, Self-sufficiency. Each colony gets a plan from its planet, hazards and resources. With Terraforming Made Easy, that includes a reachable special world (Forge, Fortress, Paradise, Hive, Death, Waste) resolved from TME's own requirements. A world is only picked if its industries, with the colony's other industries, fit the slots the colony gets at its max size (1 slot to size 3, then 2, 3 and 4 at sizes 4 to 6, plus any bonus it has), and a year of what its end conditions do (hazard on upkeep and growth, deposits) counts, so a world that keeps or adds hazards loses to one that clears them. TME's structures and the Unification Center cost more than they earn, but on a plan they get the plan's priority anyway, since they're how the world is reached. A colony with no reachable world keeps a plain plan: no world steps, only hazard clean-up. A step research blocks for now (the building the colony must add next is locked) gives up its rank, so the next step that can be built gets the most, and a step whose own building is locked passes its rank to what it's upgraded from (Smelting on the way to Refining). A new industry the plan doesn't ask for counts 30,000 a month less while the free industry slots are all the plan's industries have left.
- **Fast buildout:** after the first colony is founded, a popup offers to build it up fast: Auto, starting the fastest-paying builds (value per credit) daily with everything above a credit floor (100k, 250k, 500k or 1M; 50k-2M in the colony panel). It ranks by money only (the Money goal's weights with ground defense, fleets and ship quality worth nothing, and no plan or upgrade bonus), whatever the colony's goal and even while the threat is high, so it never builds defenses, patrols or stations. The monthly budget, per-project cap, pause on loss and new-upkeep cap don't apply; fees, the stability floor, locks and the measured-building approval do. It ends after 30 days in a row of positive colony net income, leaving the colony in Auto with its own goal. The colony panel can stop it, change the floor, or start one for any colony that loses money.
- **First switch to Approve or Auto** opens the setup page, pre-filled with the recommended settings: everything that pays for itself is on, and removals, story points and offense stay yours. **Use recommended settings** in the empire panel puts them back any time. Until setup runs, agents, emergency autonomy, research, contracts and the settlement do nothing.
- **Orbital Fusion Lamp:** it cancels Cold, Very Cold, Poor Light and Dark, but always needs 10 volatiles (a shortage adds up to +50% hazard), and on any world that isn't cold it adds heat: Hot, or Extreme Heat in place of Hot. Autopilot places it only where the cold or darkness it cancels is worth more than the heat it adds, or where the colony's plan needs Extreme Heat (Terraforming Made Easy's ore projects take it in place of extreme tectonic activity). A lamp at an Auto colony where it does neither goes back to Autopilot Storage, one a day, and the game puts the colony's old heat back.
- **Autopilot Storage:** a tab at every colony market. Colony items, AI cores, Antimatter Stabilizers and research databanks deposited there are the only ones the manager uses. The empire panel's Autopilot Storage page lists what's in it, each colony's inspection points and, with Vaults of Knowledge, the Research section.
- **Storage cheat** (Settings, **Cheats**; off, and never in the recommended settings): Autopilot Storage takes anything but ships and ambassadors, and every colony that uses stockpiles for shortages draws from it for free. Like the game's own stockpile: a colony short of a commodity in storage gets the missing amount as supply, and storage is drawn down at the game's stockpile rate for that shortage. It comes before the colony's own stockpile. The bonus is worked out every frame and taken off before every save, so turning the cheat off leaves nothing behind in the save.

## Any mix of mods

Every part has a fallback, so the manager works with any combination of the supported mods, or none:

| Part | With the mod | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| Colony plans (Terraforming Made Easy) | A reachable special world: its industries must fit the slots the colony gets at max size, and its end conditions' hazard and deposits count; TME's structures and the Unification Center get the plan's priority | A plain plan for the goal (Forge, Fortress, Paradise, Bastion, Fuel Depot, Mining Colony, Trade Hub), vanilla buildings plus hazard clean-up; with TME on but no world reachable, the same without world steps |
| Plan steps | Any step whose building or condition the game has | A step from a mod that isn't installed is dropped, so it never takes a plan rank |
| Research locks (Ashes of the Domain, Building Menu Overhaul) | Locked steps are worked toward through research; AotD's own versions count through their upgrade links | Every vanilla building is open; nothing waits on research |
| Research (Vaults of Knowledge) | Techs ranked by what colony plans need through their whole chain, then by what they unlock; a Research Facility is worth building when plans wait on research and you have none | Skipped |
| Terraforming (TME, Boggled's TASC) | Projects scored by what they add or remove, plan conditions first | Skipped; hazard clean-up steps drop out with their buildings |
| Growth | Hazard pay for the colonies it speeds up most, within a share of income; growth counts in every goal | Same (vanilla) |
| Values | Formulas for vanilla buildings, estimates for known mod buildings, probe-colony measurements for the rest | A building that can't be valued is listed in Diagnostics and never built by Auto |

Whatever a colony's plan is blocked on, it still builds whatever is worth most on its own; the plan only adds priority.

## Rules it follows

- **Only worthwhile builds:** Approve and Auto only start a build that pays for itself within about 3 years: its money and other effects, valued with the colony goal's weights, must beat its build cost spread over 3 years. They never fall back to the least-bad option, so a building with nothing to do (a Research Facility with no research, say) isn't built. The colony panel shows each option's money per month after upkeep and how its ranking is made up, and marks the ones that aren't worth building.
- **Nexerelin outposts** (Nexerelin's forward bases, which sit in an economy of their own and can't export) are left alone: nothing is built, upgraded, placed or switched there, fast buildout skips them, and their shortages don't count as your colonies'.
- **Budget:** spends within the monthly budget, per-project cap and credit reserve. Emergency defense fleets may pass the monthly budget, never the reserve. Budget types: **Automatic** (recommended: 50% of last month's net income plus 15% of the credits above the reserve at the start of the month, so a young colony that loses money still gets built and spending slows as savings fall), **Share of net income**, or **Fixed amount**. Recommended reserve: a quarter of your credits at setup, 50,000-500,000; **Pause on loss** recommended off. Each pass a colony puts up its best two upgrades and best three new builds, so when the best has to wait (budget, caps) the next one goes instead of the colony standing still. **New upkeep cap per month:** a plan step whose upkeep alone is over the whole cap (a Terraforming Made Easy structure at a big colony, say) goes as the month's first new upkeep, so it waits a month at most; before 2.21.0 it waited forever and the colony with it.
- **Surplus investment** (setting, recommended over 3,000,000; saves from before 2.21.0 get it once, with a log line): credits over the line go into one **focus world**, to make it the best colony it can be. While there's a surplus the focus world may spend it past the monthly budget and the per-project cap, its new upkeep cap is 10% of net income, its builds and terraforming count their price at a quarter (so expensive buildings that pay off count), its best-value terraforming is paid from the surplus, and research counts its plan twice and facility upkeep at a quarter. Credits already promised to production aren't surplus: the game's custom production still to build and, with Ashes of the Domain, its production orders' remaining units (Jonah's Auto Outfitting's weapon orders go there), so a weapons order is never left unpaid. Autopilot picks the Auto colony with the most to build on (size, free industry slots, deposits, habitable, hazard, waiting colony items that fit, income, a reachable special world) and keeps it unless another is clearly better; pick it yourself with **Make this the focus world** on its colony panel's Overview. The empire panel's Colonies page says how much is over the line and where it goes.
- **Fees:** convenience fees are fixed vanilla-scale amounts. Off, Low, Standard or High.
- **Removals:** buildings are only removed during terraforming, or anywhere with **Allow removals** on. The Antimatter Stabilizer conversion counts as a removal.
- **Story points:** player only by default. Options: one improvement per colony, or a monthly cap on story points spent. Each improvement at a colony costs twice the last one there (2, 4, 8, 16...), so the cheapest improvement anywhere goes first, and one that would take the month past the cap waits.
- **Terraforming:** hazards a project adds are scored, and side effects (TME removes a new condition's conflicts) are simulated in planning. A project that's a step of the colony's plan, the special world's final project included, goes past the per-project cap and the monthly terraforming budget: it's paid from your credits above the reserve, and waits (logged) until you have that much.
- **Colony items:** each item in Autopilot Storage goes into the Auto colony building where it scores best, one a day with the standard fee. With no empty building for it, it swaps in for an item at least a quarter less valuable in the same kind of building (a Pristine Nanoforge for a Corrupted one), which goes back to storage. An item no Auto colony has a building for makes a new building that would take it worth that much more: its extra output, or its value next to the vanilla items, shown as "Uses the ... waiting in Autopilot Storage" on the option.
- **AI cores:** one switch, on in the recommended settings (only cores you put in Autopilot Storage are used). Each core goes where it adds most for the colony's goal, valued the way buildings are: vanilla gives every core -1 demand (worth something only where an input is short, which also gives back a unit of output), Alpha and Beta -25% upkeep, and an Alpha +1 to each output, or in a structure its own bonus (fleet size, station strength, ground defense, accessibility, Commerce income, growth). Less the colony crisis progress it adds (see **Colony crises**): the Hegemony counts every core at a colony above size 3 (Alpha 4, Beta 2, Gamma 1; size 3 or less is free), and Pather interest from cores is the same 4/2/1, which gives a colony of size 4 or more a Pather cell once it reaches 7 (then all of its interest counts). A core that doesn't pay for that stays in storage. A better core replaces a lesser one, which goes back to storage; a core moves to another building only for 3,000 a month more. One change a day, with the standard fee.
  - **Inspection risk** (setting): the Hegemony only inspects a colony with more than 7 inspection points (any size, 180 days old). **Never inspected** (recommended since 2.14.0) keeps every colony at 7 or less. **More where defended** (what older saves get) allows more where the defenses beat the next inspection by 1.5 times (2 times while the colony's threat is high); that only pays with **Hegemony inspection orders** on Resist where defended, since complying hands the cores over. **Small colonies only** keeps cores out of colonies above size 3, so cores add no Hegemony progress at all. **No limit** ignores inspections. There's no limit once you've defeated the Hegemony's inspections, while you're in the Persean League, or when the Hegemony holds no colonies.
  - **Hegemony inspection orders** (setting): the game's default is to comply, which hands over every core at that colony (and its administrator, if an AI core). **Advise** (recommended, and what older saves get) logs each inspection coming for an Auto colony with its fleet points and the colony's defenses, so you can resist or bribe from its intel before it arrives. **Resist where defended** resists at a colony whose defenses beat it by the margin above: the Hegemony turns hostile when the task force arrives, and three beaten inspections (150, 600, then 1,400 fleet points) end them for good. **Off** does nothing.
  - A colony over its limit (its station lost, the next inspection stronger, the setting changed) sends its least useful core back to Autopilot Storage, one a day. Cores it can't move (an AI core administrator, locked buildings) are logged once, and no more go there.
  - A colony an inspection is planned against or on its way to is left as it is: the inspectors expect the cores they saw, and finding fewer disrupts every building there. A colony a Pather attack is planned against drops to under 7 Pather interest instead, one core a day, so its cell can dissolve before the attack fires.
  - While a colony's threat is high, new cores there only go into its defenses. Alpha cores only go where they can be removed cleanly (configurable).
  - Autopilot Storage lists each Auto colony's inspection points and limit; the colony panel's Overview shows that colony's.
- **Administrators:** hired, placed or moved only where they add more than they cost. What one adds at a colony: Industrial Planning's +1 to everything its industries make (valued like any output, so a big, productive colony gains far more than a small one), plus the stability from your colony limit (each colony you govern past it costs every colony you govern 2 stability; stability is only money below 5, where each point is 20% of the colony's income). That is set against the salary (vanilla: 2,500 a month unskilled, 20,000 with a skill, 10% while unassigned) and, when hiring, the hiring cost spread over three years. An idle administrator worth less than their salary everywhere stays unassigned and the log says so; dismissing them is yours. **Colony limit:** while you govern more colonies than your limit yourself (founding a 3rd, 4th or 5th colony), or Autopilot made a colony autonomous in the past, an idle administrator is placed, or one is hired, whatever the money says: each colony past the limit costs every colony you govern 2 stability, and a new colony needs it. That hire may go past the monthly budget, never below the credit reserve. The log says when none is for hire, when you employ as many as you can, or when hiring is off. One is moved to another Auto colony when they'd add at least 5,000 a month (or a quarter of their salary) more there. The colony panel's Overview shows its administrator's salary against what they add. Hiring is on in the recommended settings; the 30-day arrival delay is optional.
- **Ambassadors (Industrial Evolution):** focus setting (worst relations first, strongest factions, nearest neighbors, or priority list only), per-faction priority (High, Normal, Ignore), and a "stop raising at" reputation level.
- **Siege:** a colony that's blockaded, has hostile fleets at the planet, has hostiles in its system that outweigh its defenses (the same rule as the emergency defense fleet), or has a hostile raid, invasion or blockade on its way, is under siege until that ends (checked daily). Meanwhile Autopilot only builds defenses there (nothing during a fast buildout), and leaves its items, AI cores, administrators, policies, station augments and terraforming as they are; hazard pay and free port still switch off in an emergency. The log says when a siege starts and ends, and the colony panel says why.
- **Attacks on your colonies (Nexerelin Derelict Empire start only):** that start (`$nex_scenarioId` derelict_empire or derelict_empire_v2) gives every faction 0.8 of an invasion's points on day 0, and a blockade needs 0.75, so blockades and raids come within weeks while your colony is new. In that start only, a settings group of this name appears and the recommended settings turn both on (at most 4 fleets, one at a time 90 days apart); in any other game neither is shown or applied and the game's own values stay.
  - **Pirate and crisis fleets per system:** Hostile Activity keeps up to 11 pirate, Pather and other crisis fleets in a system with your colonies (`maxHostileActivityFleetsPerSystem`), the most for a big colony at low stability. Choose at most 8, 6, 4 or 2. The game's own value is kept and put back when it's off, or when another game is loaded; a lower value another mod set is never raised.
  - **Nexerelin invasions, raids and blockades:** Nexerelin waits only 60 days between attacks on you, and a blockade lasts 120, so they stack up. One at a time, 30, 60, 90 or 180 days apart, holds Nexerelin's own per-faction cooldown (`$nex_recentlyTargetedMilitary` on your faction) while any raid, invasion or blockade is on its way to or at one of your colonies, and for that many days after. IX Revival's blockades come through Nexerelin. Nexerelin's hard mode ignores the cooldown. IX's own difficulty stays in its LunaLib menu.
- **Colony crises:** the game's crisis meter (Hostile Activity) gains every faction's progress each month, at most 60, times your best military structure (Patrol HQ x0.9, Military Base x0.7, High Command x0.5). At 500 the next crisis is picked, weighted by each faction's share; at 600 it fires and the meter falls back to 0-400. A month with no progress empties it. Autopilot prices progress at about 640 a month per point (a crisis roughly every 467 points, each held to cost 300,000):
  - AI cores pay for the Hegemony progress and Pather interest they add (above). A Patrol HQ, Military Base or High Command that would become your best military structure counts what it slows. Fuel Production counts the Sindrian Diktat progress it adds (the Diktat counts your largest fuel output from 4, once a fuel producer is size 4 or more).
  - The colony the coming crisis is likely to hit (the one it picked, or your system where that faction's activity is strongest) plans with Security until it's over, so it builds stations, patrols and ground defense. Raids that meet in-system defenses about 1.34 times their strength while you're away abort, and that ends pirate, Pather, Diktat and Tri-Tachyon crises for good. The log says once which crisis is coming and where.
  - A coming Pather attack pulls cores from its target (above). A coming Nexerelin police raid on free ports closes the free ports of Auto colonies that would count (size + drugs + weapons - 2, from 5).
  - Deals, fights and memberships stay yours. The empire panel's **Colony crises** page shows the meter, the coming crisis and its likely target, your military multiplier, each faction's monthly progress with what drives it, how it ends and what Autopilot does about it, and the Hegemony's inspections so far. Nothing skips the game's rules: no avert helpers or flags.
- **Hazard pay:** every goal wants growth: bigger colonies make more, get more industry slots and reach their plans. Hazard pay adds a colony's hazard penalty back plus 5 growth points a month, at 1,000 credits a point, and a colony needs 300 x 2^(size-3) points for its next size. Auto colonies are ranked by how much faster it makes them grow (its points against their own growth; a colony that barely grows gains most), and the best ones get it while it speeds them up by at least **Hazard pay where growth is at least** (25%) and all of it fits within **Hazard pay limit, all colonies** (30% of net income before hazard pay). The more you earn, the more colonies get it. One already paying keeps it with a little leeway, so it doesn't flip; it goes off in a money emergency, at max size, or when the budget goes to colonies it speeds up more. The log gives the growth and the months to the next size with and without it. When one colony's hazard pay costs more than the whole budget, the log says so with both numbers. Saves from before 2.20.0 still on the old default of 10% move to 30% once (logged).
- **Threat response:** a colony is planned as Security once its threat estimate reaches 70%. It goes back to its own goal only after the estimate falls below 65% and at least 60 days have passed, so free port, hazard pay and edicts don't flip back and forth at the line. The estimate uses hostile factions nearby (at most 60% on their own), the Hostile Activity crisis, and raid, blockade, Pather cell and rebellion conditions. The state is kept in the save.
- **Log:** "waits for budget", "waits for next month", unavailable edicts and similar notes are logged once per save, not after every load. They can be logged again after the thing they reported happens.
- **Agents (Nexerelin):** setting **Agents** (under **Agents (Nexerelin)**): Off before setup, Defense recommended, or Defense and offense. Defense is what Nexerelin's **Internal security mode** does, checked daily and paid from the budget, so Autopilot's agents don't need that mode; with no rebellion, Pather cell, hidden pirate base or enemy operation at your colonies they wait, and the Agents section says why. Only agents Autopilot hired, or ones you hand over with **Let Autopilot use it** on the empire panel's Mod features page, get orders; **Take it back** returns one. Each agent only gets operations its type can run (Nexerelin's agentConfig): Raise Relations needs a Negotiator or Hybrid, Sabotage Industry a Saboteur, and the defensive operations take any type.
  - Nexerelin has no counter-intelligence and no bonus for an agent parked at a colony, so defense runs the operations Nexerelin's own automatic mode runs, in its order: Counter-Insurgency on a rebellion, Infiltrate Cell on an active Pather cell, Find Pirate Base for a hidden base, each at the closest colony that needs it. Then Raise Relations with a faction that has operations running against your colonies while it isn't at war with you: above Neutral its Strategic AI can't start them and drops the running ones.
  - Offense only targets factions already hostile to you; never allies, a faction in a ceasefire, or your commissioner, since a detected operation can start a war. It sabotages the military industry of a market an invasion or raid against you comes from, else Nexerelin's own military pick, at 60 percent estimated success or better, alert 0.2 or lower, one operation per enemy faction.
  - Hiring (on unless turned off): a level-1 agent for 12,000, Nexerelin's own price for a chosen specialization. Hybrid first; a Saboteur when offense is on. Never past **Agents to keep** (Auto: 1, or 2 with offense) or Nexerelin's agent limit, and only while there's work for one that none of your own idle agents of a suitable type could do; then the log names that agent instead. Nexerelin pays the 2,000+ monthly salary itself.
  - Operation costs and hiring come out of the monthly budget, never the credit reserve. The Agents section of the empire panel's Mod features page lists every agent you have (type, level, what it's doing, whether Autopilot gives its orders) and the enemy operations running against your colonies, which the game itself doesn't show. Only person ids are saved, so the save loads without Nexerelin.
- **Emergency autonomy (Nexerelin):** autonomy is your call; Autopilot never grants it (since 2.17.0). Setting **Emergency autonomy**: Off before setup, or Recommend (recommended). In a money emergency (net income below zero with under 3 months of it left above the credit reserve), Recommend logs which colony a year old or more loses the most, and the empire panel's Mod features page has **Grant autonomy** for it: you get 25% of its profit or loss and it no longer counts against your colony limit. Colonies you make autonomous are never touched. Going past your colony limit gets an administrator instead (above). Colonies an older Autopilot made autonomous are taken back, one a day, as soon as there's no money emergency, taking one back wouldn't start one, and there's room under your colony limit or an idle administrator to put there (installed in the same step); without Nexerelin's 2 unrest, since they weren't your choice. The Mod features page lists them with **Take it back now**.
- **Ashes of the Domain:** research locks and upgrade paths live in Building Menu Overhaul's build menu, so the manager asks it the same questions the menu does:
  - Nothing locked by research or hidden in the menu is built or upgraded. If those rules can't be read, nothing is built or upgraded while Building Menu Overhaul is enabled, and the log says why.
  - Buildings AotD reaches by upgrading (Farming from Monoculture Plots, Orbital Works from Heavy Industry, Megaport from Spaceport) are offered as upgrades, started the way the menu starts them. A structure that upgrades into an industry needs a free industry slot.
  - AotD's starter buildings are supported with estimated effects: Monoculture Plots, Extractive Operation, Smelting, Light Production, Trade Outpost. A plan step such as Farming credits the starter it upgrades from, and an upgraded version (Artisanal Farming) counts as done.
  - Buildings the Unification Center checks by exact id aren't upgraded into AotD versions while that plan is open.
  - Plan steps that are still locked show what they need, e.g. "(locked: ...)".
  - AI cores: AotD versions of a building are valued like any building that makes things (an Alpha adds +1 output), and AotD's Commerce gets the vanilla Commerce Alpha bonus.
- **Industry synergies (Ashes of the Domain, Vaults of Knowledge):** building combinations on one colony (Ore-To-Core: Refining with Mining; Defensive Network: star fortress, Heavy Batteries and High Command; Rapid Transportation: Spaceport with Waystation; and 16 more).
  - A build's estimated value includes the synergies it starts or ends, and for Spaceport, Waystation, Maglev and Daedalus Array the efficiency they add to synergies already running.
  - Effects are modelled from VoK 5.0.7's code. Most apply in full whatever the efficiency (+2 metals, x1.5 ground defense); only some scale with it (accessibility, Resort Center income, ship quality, Syndicate Line income). Pather interest counts as a small stability effect.
  - Only synergies researched at that colony count. The colony panel shows its efficiency, the active synergies and those one building away.
- **Trade contracts (Ashes of the Domain, Theory of Toolbox):** monthly deliveries from your surplus, paid at 2-5x the export cut. Three missed months end a contract and cost merchant renown. Mode Advisor (before setup), Auto (recommended) or Off.
  - An offer is only taken when ToT's own figures (your production against your demand plus every contract you hold) leave 15% to spare, and it pays more than the exports those goods would have earned. Up to 3 per day in Auto, best gain first.
  - ToT only delivers from colonies with a spaceport and accessibility above 0, and a blockade takes up to 60% off. So the surplus of a colony without one, or under siege, doesn't count as spare.
  - The empire panel lists held contracts, what they're short of, and the month's offers with pay, gain over exporting and rewards; Advisor gives each fillable offer an Accept button.
  - Commodities held contracts are short of count as shortages when scoring buildings, so colonies that would make them score higher. A missed delivery is logged.
  - Programs you issue yourself, freezing and terminating are left to you.
- **Coronal Hypershunt colony (Ashes of the Domain, Vaults of Knowledge):** AotD caps it at size 2 and allows no buildings, so nothing is built, upgraded, placed or switched there (no items, free port or hazard pay). Its port, patrol HQ and star fortress are hidden.
  - **Range:** the Coronal Wormhole's range costs 20,000 credits and 2 purified transplutonics a month per 10 light-years past the first. Autopilot keeps it at the smallest that reaches every Hypershunt Receiver in a system with one of your colonies. If you set the range yourself, it's left alone until you click "Let Autopilot manage it".
  - **Restoration:** each section is restored through a contract you issue. Every month it takes what your exports can spare and bills 10% of base price for what it gets; a shortfall only slows it. The Coronal Collector comes first, once Hypershunt Energy Distribution (receivers) is researched. The Coronal Wormhole follows when a receiver serving your colonies is past 10 light-years.
  - Auto starts a restoration only when every resource it needs is in surplus, the whole cost fits the per-project cap, the first month fits the budget, and the section's upkeep fits the new-upkeep cap.
  - Setting **Coronal Hypershunt range and restoration** (under Ashes of the Domain): Follow colony mode (default: an Auto hypershunt acts, Approve logs the recommendation once), Recommend only, Off.
  - Receivers are built in person, so the empire panel lists systems where one would pay off: your colonies there, the distance, the range it needs, free stable locations, and how many Coronal Hypershunt Taps you hold.
- **Pluto Mining Station and Nidavelir Complex (Vaults of Knowledge):** claimed by colonizing their planets. Same setting as the hypershunt (**Megastructures**), and the same restoration gates. Settings you change yourself are left alone until you click "Let Autopilot manage it".
  - **Pluto:** the laser runs at full strength (each 10% step costs 5,000 a month for about 285,000 worth of ore). Every unit of ore it digs goes to a restored forge: the Purification Core, except what the Fusion Forge needs to cover your metals and transplutonics shortages. Auto restores the Optic Command Nexus, then the Purification Core, then the Fusion Forge.
  - **Nidavelir:** manpower goes into the Nexus Core one point at a time while its four inputs are fully supplied, and comes back off when they're short, since a shortage is taken off every restored section's output. There's a 30-day wait before adding again, and a cap of 20 (where upkeep reaches 0). Auto restores the Nexus Core. The other sections' restoration, manpower and automation are left to you.
- **Research (Vaults of Knowledge):** mode Advisor (before setup), Auto (recommended) or Off.
  - Each tech you can start is valued by what it unlocks. The buildings are scored by the same engine that picks builds: the candidate techs are marked researched for one scoring pass over your colonies, then put back. Non-building effects come from VoK's code: +1 output, hazard -10%, upkeep x0.9, and hypershunt access. Half the best tech each one leads to is added.
  - **Colony plans come first:** every open plan step that research locks (Building Menu Overhaul's gates, VoK's rewards, Terraforming Made Easy's own tech for each structure), and the buildings it's upgraded from, is traced to the whole chain of techs it still needs. The step's plan value is shared along that chain by research days, so the first tech of a three-tech chain to a Terrestrial Engine counts, not only the last.
  - Ranked by a year of that value, less databanks and the facility upkeep it adds, per research day. The empire panel's Autopilot Storage page lists what each colony's plan waits on (techs, databanks and days) and the best six techs with a Start button.
  - Auto starts the best one when nothing is running or queued, yours or its own: while you have research queued it waits (the panel says so). It picks techs that cost databanks and AI cores (the cores from Autopilot Storage, counted in the cost at 2,500/7,500/20,000 for Gamma/Beta/Alpha); techs that use colony items are left to you. Databanks are the scarce part: while the best plan tech is short of them, the ones you have go only to techs some plan needs, and the log says which tech is next, for what, and how many databanks it needs. After 90 days of that, the best tech you can afford goes ahead anyway, so research never sits idle for good. Hazmat Equipment and Aquatic Agriculture count the growth their hazard cut gives colonies below max size, and the facility upkeep a tech adds counts your facilities' AI cores (alpha x0.5, beta x0.75, gamma x0.9). Plan techs aren't held back by the new-upkeep cap (research upkeep ends with the tech); a tech over the cap no longer stops cheaper ones.
  - Autopilot Storage is research's storage too: when a tech starts (Auto, or the Start button), whatever the Research Facility tab is short of (databanks, an Alpha core, colony items) is moved in from Autopilot Storage, then databanks from your colonies' Storage tabs (VoK puts a research site's databanks there while the facility is down), then from your fleet's cargo. Nothing moves unless that covers the whole shortfall. Sophia Anderson, if you have her, becomes head of R&D before paying, since she takes one databank off each tech. Your queue is never touched.

  - Centralized Urban Core: upkeep x0.8 except military; non-extractive output +min(2, size/3) per line; farming and mining -min(3, size-2).

  - Bore City: Mining and Extractive Operation output x1.7. Its x1.3 build cost hooks a Building Menu Overhaul method that this BMO version never calls, so no surcharge applies in your game.
  - Archeo-Site: Tech-Mining x1.7. Its build whitelist is enforced through BMO's hidden list.
  - Floating City: each building adds 3-5 thruster fuel demand, and a fuel shortage lowers the city until it's lost. Nothing is built while fuel would run short, and a falling city is logged once per stage.
  - The colony panel names the colony's style and what it does to builds.
- **AotD colony items:** the Tenebrian Nanoforge, Synchrotron, Atmospheric Driver and Catalytic Core, and the Turing Engine, are placed from Autopilot Storage like vanilla items. The Coronal Hypershunt Tap (AotD uses it to build receivers), the AquaOrb (no effect) and the Hyperdimensional Processor (research) never are.

- **Theory of Toolbox's economy threads:** the daily pass waits while ToT's background workers are reading markets, or a save is under way, for at most a day.
- **Left to you (AotD):** Bifrost gates (where to put them is a strategic call, and at default weights a gate rarely pays its 20,000 a month), Grand Wonders (one-off choices; Elysium deletes items and cores when it finishes), black-site projects and custom production (ships and items you pick), and changing a management style.
- **Any mod (generic):**
  - Deposit conditions a mod registers in the game's own deposit tables are scored like vanilla ones, for Mining, Farming and any other deposit industry.
  - Any mod's colony item that registers an effect in the game's item table is placed from Autopilot Storage. It's moved as the exact stack object, since some mods keep state in it. Items without a known value are valued by the output they add.
  - Never placed: the Coronal Hypershunt Tap and AquaOrb (AotD), the Hyperdimensional Processor, and JaydeePiracy's Planetkiller and Lobster Eggs (+1 lobster for +8 Pather interest).
  - Without Grand.Colonies, nothing new is built past 12 buildings, because the colony screen can't show a 13th.
  - Tech-Mining's value falls as its ruins deplete.
- **Terraforming & Station Construction (Boggled):** terraforming projects cost only time (200 days, or 400 for a planet type change), so each one TASC offers is valued by the conditions it leaves (hazard, deposits, habitability, as for Terraforming Made Easy).
  - Auto starts the best project when none is running; Approve logs it once; the colony panel lists the best four with Start. A running project is never cancelled.
  - The Atmosphere Processor and Stellar Reflector Array are built when a worthwhile project waits only for them. The Reflector is also valued for its solar array; Autopilot never demolishes one, since that removes the array for good.
  - Estimates for Domed Cities (hazards suppressed, ground defense x0.05), Genelab (pollution removal, lobster pens), CHAMELEON (decivilization removal), Mesozoic Park and the Kletka Simulator (AI cores).

  - Drinks are valued at a quarter of base price, since few factions buy them. Other colonies' demand for drinks has no penalty, so it isn't counted as a shortage.
- **Volkov Industrial Conglomerate:** viral contamination (from Nexerelin bombardments) shrinks a colony until it's lost. The anti-EVC treatment comes first: tier 1, then tier 2 to stop the shrinking, then tier 3 at +25 relations with VIC to cancel it. VIC's soil nanites go only where farmland is vanilla (other farmland crashes them) and genetech arrives.

- **Roider Union:** Dives and Union HQ mine deposits within 1 or 6 light-years (each system's void resource and the colony's own deposits), which a probe colony can't see.
- **Unknown Skies and What We Left Behind:** Luminescent Clouds (+3 volatiles) and Fractured World (+5 ore and transplutonic ore) add straight to Mining. Water worlds are read from the game's own list, which these mods extend.
- **Astral Ascension:** a colony without a planet crashes past size 10 with it, so hazard pay never pushes one there.
- **Checked, nothing to add:** Better Colonies (its slot works through the game's slot count), Underworld, Supply Forging, LOST_SECTOR, Kon's Player Faction Bundles, Galactic Constellate, Tri-Tachyon Expansion, Luddic Enhancement, Knights of Ludd, Interstellar Imperium, Hillock, Hazard Mining (the probe covers its building), Hiver Swarm and Adversary (their attacks come through the colony crisis and expeditions the threat estimate already counts).
- **Hidden buildings:** a building the game hides (megastructure defenses, the hypershunt's port) is never upgraded, improved or given an item or AI core, and isn't listed under Locks. A hidden station's augments are left to you.
- **Mod buildings:** a building with no formula or estimate is measured: it's built on a throwaway probe colony (your colony's size and vanilla deposits, no planet or location) and the difference is read: commodities supplied and demanded, stability, accessibility, income and upkeep multipliers, ground defense, fleet size and patrols, ship quality. It's then scored like any other building. Upgrades measure both buildings on the same probe and score the difference.
  - Effects that only happen over time (monthly items, spawned fleets) aren't seen, so measured buildings score low rather than high.
  - A building whose code fails on the probe, or that registers scripts or intel when built, is "can't be measured" and never acted on; anything it added is removed.

  - Founding and facilities use the same budget, per-project cap and fees as colonies.
- **Station augments (Station Augments):** installing costs nothing and can be undone, so the manager picks the set of known augments worth the most within each station's augment points (AP).

Economy Conversion is valued as the best industry waiting for a slot.

The log says "(threat is high: defense first)" and "(threat over: output again)".
  - Mining Station needs Station Refitting (and Nexerelin) and isn't scored yet.

  - Installed augments are only replaced when the new set is worth at least 10% (and 300 per month) more, so wobbling estimates don't swap them back and forth.
  - Left to you: augments that use up an item (Shrouded, Threat), ones that give AP back for a drawback (Extreme or Special Modifications), and Economy Conversion while the colony plans for Security. Economy Conversion is never removed once added, since its industry slot may be in use.
- **Unsupported content:** mod content the manager can't support or measure is never acted on. It's analyzed and reported (see Diagnostics).

## Diagnostics

- Every load scans all buildings, conditions and items, and rates each **Supported**, **Measured** (effects read on a probe colony; listed with what was measured), **Analyzed** or **Quarantined**.
- An error in any mod code during planning quarantines that building.
- Output goes to:
  - the empire panel's **Diagnostics** section
  - `[ColonyAutopilot]` lines in `starsector-core/starsector.log`
  - `saves/common/colony_autopilot_diagnostics.json.data` (full report with probe results and errors)
- **Jonah's mods** (top of the Diagnostics page): Colony Manager and each of Jonah's other mods that's installed (Jonah's Auto Outfitting, for one), each with its version, its warnings and errors this session (with the latest one), and its own status line (the others write theirs to sector memory as `$jonahs_status_<mod id>` on every load, so no mod needs another's classes), plus the credits promised to production that surplus investment keeps back.
- **Why nothing is built:** when an Auto colony has nothing worth starting, `starsector.log` gets one `[ColonyAutopilot] <colony>: nothing worth starting` line a month with its best three options, how each score is made up (worth, plan, follow-through, price, upkeep), and the best one that only lacks a free industry slot.
- Outside the game, the author's `jtdiag.ps1` (not shipped) checks Colony Manager and Jonah's other mods at once: built vs installed jars, repos and releases, enabled mods and dependencies, install watchers, every `starsector.log` for their warnings, errors and "nothing worth starting" lines, and the newest save (which of their classes it needs, Colony Manager's settings, waits and log).

## Staying working through mod updates

So many mods are covered that some will update. These five safeguards keep an update from quietly breaking Autopilot:

1. **Health check at load.** Each integration knows its mod, the version it was written against, the ids it relies on and the names it reads. Diagnostics shows each one's status:
   - **OK**
   - **Newer version, all found:** it keeps working, and its estimates are trusted a little less.
   - **Partly off:** the parts whose ids are gone can't act.
   - **Off:** its code can't be read, so it does nothing.
2. **Mod values read live.** Where a mod exposes its numbers (Roider's mining tables and ranges, What We Left Behind's ore bonus, AotD's restoration rate), Autopilot uses the mod's own values. Its copies are only a fallback. A value that changed is logged once and listed in Diagnostics.
3. **Calibration from your colonies.** Once a week, every plain building you have (working, with no AI core, item or improvement) is compared with Autopilot's prediction: upkeep for every building type, and output for buildings scored from estimates. The ratio is smoothed (70% old, 30% new), capped at 0.5x to 2x, used once a type has two readings, and kept in the save. Estimates follow a mod's rebalance this way.
4. **Margins for uncertain picks.** The game's own formulas count in full. Probe measurements count at 90%, and estimates at 85% (92% once calibrated), minus 5% on a mod version an integration wasn't written for. So an estimate must beat a known option by about 18% to be picked. In Auto, a pick scored from an estimate that costs over half the per-project cap waits for your approval.
5. **Stopping actions that don't work.** After each action that reaches into the game or another mod (a build, an item install, a research or terraforming start, a megastructure setting, a restoration, a contract), Autopilot checks that it took effect. Three failures in a row pause that action for the session, with a log line and a Diagnostics entry. Reloading tries again.

## Building from source

Source is in `src/`. It compiles against the game and optional mod jars with JDK 17:

```
javac --release 17 -cp "<core>/starfarer.api.jar;<core>/starfarer_obf.jar;<core>/fs.common_obf.jar;<core>/log4j-1.2.9.jar;<core>/json.jar;<core>/lwjgl.jar;<core>/lwjgl_util.jar;<TME>/TerraformingMadeEasy.jar;<IndEvo>/jars/IndEvo.jar;<Nexerelin>/jars/ExerelinCore.jar" -d build (all .java files under src)
jar cf jars/jonahtweaks.jar -C build data
```


The game's script class loader refuses any mod class that names `java.lang.reflect` or file classes ("File access and reflection are not allowed to scripts"), and desktop checks don't see that. All reflection goes through `CARefl`, which reaches `getMethod`, `invoke`, `getField`, `get` and `set` through method handles and never names the reflection classes. `check_banned.ps1` fails the build if any class refers to a class the game's loader bans. `JTLoaderCheck` loads every class under that loader and runs `CARefl`.

`CATerraform`, `CAIndEvo`, `CACourier`, `CADefense`, `CASettlement` and `CAAugments` reference optional mods. They're only called when that mod is enabled, so the mod runs without them. `CASettlement` and `CAAugments` also need the Kotlin runtime (LazyLib) and LunaLib on the compile classpath.

The author's build checks prove any combination works: jdeps shows only those classes reference each optional mod, every caller checks `isModEnabled`, and the jar links under `-Xverify:all` against all 32 combinations of the compiled-in optional mods. `CASynergies`, `CAContracts`, `CAHypershunt`, `CAMegastructures`, `CAResearch` and `CADevelopment` reach Vaults of Knowledge and Theory of Toolbox through reflection only (via `CARefl`), like `CAAotD`; if a name can't be found they're left out and the log says why. `JTAotDEconLinkCheck.java` checks those names; run it with `-XX:+UnlockDiagnosticVMOptions -XX:-BytecodeVerificationRemote` like the game's own launch flags, since some VoK classes refer to Sins of the Past classes that aren't installed. An offline check covers the augment choice (knapsack against brute force, swap margin, shrunk budget, incompatible pairs). `CAAotD` reaches Building Menu Overhaul through reflection only, so it needs no jar at compile time.

The colony-mod integrations (`CATasc`, `CAGrand`, `CABrewery`, `CAMods`) use literal ids, reflection or method handles only, so no jar is needed. TASC is reached through `MethodHandles.findStatic`, because plain reflection on its `boggledTools` needs Illustrated Entities' classes. `JTColonyModsLinkCheck.java` checks those names against the TASC and LunaLib jars.
